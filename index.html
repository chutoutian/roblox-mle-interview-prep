01How PhD-level loops actually run2 sections
Chapter link ↗

This is not the SWE loop. For research scientist / applied scientist / research engineer, the structure is fairly consistent across companies:

text
Recruiter screen
   ↓
Technical or research phone screen  (30–60 min)
   often blended: a bit of coding + project deep-dive + foundational ML
   ↓
Virtual onsite loop  (4–6 rounds)
   ├─ RESEARCH PRESENTATION / JOB TALK       30–45 min + Q&A   ← often the highest-signal round
   ├─ Paper deep-dive × 2–3                   different interviewers, different subdomains
   ├─ ML breadth                              rapid-fire fundamentals across the field
   ├─ ML depth                                your specialty, pushed to the edge
   ├─ Coding                                  LeetCode-medium, or an ML/domain implementation
   ├─ ML / system design                      "design a video quality system", "design transcoding"
   └─ Behavioral / bar-raiser                 weighted heavily at Amazon, Meta, Netflix
   ↓
Hiring committee → team match

Track distinctions worth knowing, because they change what's tested:

TrackWeight
Research ScientistJob talk + paper depth dominate. Publication record matters. Coding is a floor, not a differentiator.
Research EngineerBalanced. Strong coding + can you turn research into a running system.
Applied ScientistML breadth + system design + business framing. Amazon's pillars: ML Breadth, ML Depth, Live Coding, Science Application/Design, Bar Raiser.
ML/Video EngineerCoding and systems dominate; research is context.

Apply to the track that matches your strength. Yours is unusually broad — you have both papers and shipped systems — so you can credibly go for any of the first three. Research Engineer and Applied Scientist are probably the highest-conversion given the systems work.

The job talk is the round people under-prepare#

30–45 minutes on your own work, to a room of researchers who will interrupt. Standard advice that actually matters:

  • Pick one project, not five. Depth beats survey. A talk covering everything signals nothing.
  • Motivation for the first 5 minutes. Half the room isn't in your subfield. If they don't understand why the problem is hard before you show a method, the rest is wasted.
  • Say your limitations out loud. The single strongest credibility move. If you don't name them, someone will find one and it'll look like you didn't know.
  • Expect hostile-sounding questions. That's engagement, not rejection. Being able to say "that's a real weakness, here's what it costs and here's what I'd do" scores higher than a defense.
  • Have a backup slide for every number on your headline slide.

Your best job-talk candidates, in order: Trinity (cleanest non-obvious insight, real user study, systems + perception), MARS (same, plus production integration), or the TikTok VQA work if the team is ML-leaning. GenStream/AquaScope if the team is codec/generative-model focused.

Paper deep-dive rounds#

2–3 separate interviewers, deliberately probing different subdomains to test whether your depth is narrow or broad. Prep:

  • Read 5–10 recent papers in your area deeply enough to critique, not summarize. For you: DCVC-FM, Cosmos/MAGVIT-v2/TiTok tokenizers, Q-Align + the NTIRE UGC VQA line, BasicVSR++, recent ABR work.
  • For each of your own papers: what's the baseline, why is it the right baseline, what breaks it, what would you do with 10× the compute, what did reviewers object to.
  • Know the related work you didn't cite and why.

04Company-flavored notes4 sections
Chapter link ↗

Not exhaustive — these are the flavors that change your answers. Map any company you interview at onto an archetype above, then check whether one of these notes applies.

Streaming platforms (Netflix, Prime Video, YouTube, Meta, TikTok, Disney/Hulu, Roku, Bilibili) The recurring question is a cost model: encode once, deliver N times. That single relation decides how much compute a video deserves, which codecs to support, how many ladder rungs, and whether per-shot optimization pays. Netflix specifically: VMAF internals and its failure modes, Dynamic Optimizer / convex hull, AV1 at ~30% of streams, film grain synthesis, subjective-study design. Expect direct technical debate — push back with evidence.

Silicon & systems (Nvidia, Qualcomm, Apple, Intel, AMD, Samsung, Ambarella) Hardware/software boundary reasoning. Why fixed-function blocks exist, what they cost you in flexibility, zero-copy pipelines, roofline/bandwidth reasoning — all at the pipeline level, which is where your experience actually is. Nvidia: they built the Cosmos Tokenizer you use, so have opinions about it. If kernel-level CUDA comes up, the honest answer is the right one — "I work at the pipeline and model level; I've optimized NVDEC→inference→NVENC dataflow, not written kernels" — and then redirect to what you have optimized. Screen for roles where that's a gate before investing time. Apple: on-device constraints, ANE op coverage, CoreML conversion, color management, Low-Latency HLS, MV-HEVC for spatial video.

At-scale infra (Google/YouTube, Meta, Amazon) Custom silicon exists because transcoding is a top-line datacenter cost — Google's Argos VCU (20–33× compute efficiency, gen-2 does AV1), Meta's MSVP (~9× vs libx264). Expect "design transcoding for 500 hours uploaded per minute" and "which videos get the expensive encode." Amazon adds the Bar Raiser and Leadership Principles; Meta weights behavioral heavily.

Mid-size video infra (Bitmovin, Mux, Agora, LiveKit, V-Nova, Visionular, Dolby) Smaller loops, less ritual, far more depth on the actual product. You'll talk to people who ship encoders or SFUs for a living, so vague answers get caught immediately — but a real systems background counts for more here than a publication list. Often the highest-leverage applications for someone with your profile, and usually faster.

Foundation-model labs (DeepMind, FAIR, OpenAI, Runway, Luma, Twelve Labs, ByteDance Seed) Publication record and research taste dominate. The job talk is the round. Expect to defend methodology hard and to be asked what you'd do with 100× compute.

Cross-cutting: what every one of them probes#

  1. Can you reason about a tradeoff you weren't handed? Every strong answer here is "it depends on X, and here's the curve." Latency vs quality, rate vs distortion vs perception, FLOPs vs bandwidth, fidelity vs realism.
  2. Do you know why, not just what? "HEVC is 50% better" is weak. "Larger flexible partitioning, better MV prediction, 8-tap interpolation, and SAO — and the partitioning is why the gain is bigger at 4K" is strong.
  3. Have you shipped and measured, or only read? Say how you measured, what confounded it, and what you'd trust.
  4. Do you volunteer the limitation? Naming your method's weakness before they find it is the single strongest credibility move available.

Weak spots to shore up#

  • Classical codec internals at source level — you're strong at the systems/ML layer. Be ready for "how does CABAC context selection work" or "walk me through x264's mode decision."
  • Coding fluency under time pressure — the local .py files are good domain practice; also drill plain LeetCode-medium, since it's a floor in every loop.
  • A crisp, non-defensive story for why neural codecs aren't deployed — you'll be tested on this wherever traditional codecs pay the bills.
  • The job talk itself — 30–45 min, rehearsed out loud, timed, with backup slides. Most PhD candidates under-invest here relative to how much it counts.
  • Behavioral stories — 5–6 STAR narratives (04 §8).

Four-week plan#

Two parallel tracks, every day. Content alone doesn't get you hired at this level; the talk does a disproportionate amount of the work. Running them in parallel also means the talk gets four weeks of iteration instead of being crammed at the end — which is the actual difference between a good talk and a rehearsed-once talk.

Job talk build checklist#

Track B produces this. Check it before any onsite.

  • One-sentence thesis you can say without notes
  • First 5 minutes are motivation, comprehensible to someone outside your subfield
  • 8–12 content slides. More than 15 means you're surveying, not going deep.
  • One project, not five. A talk covering everything signals nothing.
  • Limitations stated out loud by you, before anyone asks
  • A backup slide for every number on your headline slide
  • 20-question Q&A bank, each answerable in under 60 seconds
  • Rehearsed: alone (recorded) → labmate (interrupting) → outside-subfield listener
  • Timed to land 5 minutes short — you will run long when interrupted
  • Opening 2 minutes memorized, so nerves can't touch it

Scaling the plan#

  • Two weeks instead of four: keep every Track B day, halve Track A by skipping the "full drill cold" days and reading §-summaries instead of full sections. Content is recoverable in an interview; a bad talk isn't.
  • Six-plus weeks: add a second job talk on a different project (Trinity and the TikTok VQA work), so you can match the talk to the team. Add a weekly full-drill rotation across all three files.
  • Interview already scheduled inside two weeks: skip to Week 4, then backfill Track A in whatever order matches the team's archetype.
